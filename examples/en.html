ENGLISH OVERVIEW

One Old Document at a Time
고문서 한 장 · Gomunseo Han Jang

A public series that reads Korean historical documents from the Academy of Korean Studies' Korean Old Documents Archive (한국고문서자료관, archive.aks.ac.kr) one sheet at a time — full transcription, modern Korean translation, explanatory notes, and a short story built on the document's own words — and turns each into a video, an online exhibition, a classroom worksheet and card news. The pages are in Korean; this page explains what is here.

What you can explore

Gallery

All episodes with Reels (9:16) and wide (16:9) videos with Korean subtitles. Open an episode for the full reading: transcription, translation, notes, editing before/after.

Full-text search

Search all transcriptions, translations and notes — try a single Chinese character such as 乘馹 ("ride the post-horse").

Integrated analysis

Documents by century (15th c.: 5, 16th c.: 46, 17th c.: 132, 18th c.: 158, 19th c.: 143, 20th c.: 36), type, region, emotional framing, reading difficulty, quotations and questions. Data as JSON, CSV and a text-only ZIP.

Timeline · Document types

Every dated document in one chronology, and a guide to 175 document types (appointment letters, exam diplomas, household registers, sale deeds, letters…) with how to read each.

People index

210 people named in the documents, in Korean alphabetical order — each with their documents, mentions in other episodes' translations and notes, and those who appear across several episodes.

Quoted lines · Questions

Every source line quoted in the stories, searchable; and the open questions each episode leaves for readers, printable as discussion cards.

Make your own

The whole series was produced with an open-source Claude plugin, aks-archive-harvester: harvest → curate → edit → mediate → package. A step-by-step tutorial runs the full pipeline on three collections.

Curatorial note

Why the series exists, how documents were chosen (a human voice in one line; diversity of collections, periods and status), the version history and the principles kept.

Where the documents come from

Episodes by kind of holder: 127 from lineage families, 10 from museums, 8 from Confucian academies (seowon), 5 from villages, 4 from Buddhist temples, 4 from local Confucian schools (hyanggyo), across 12 provinces and cities of Korea. Each page links to the original images in the archive's viewer.

Two kinds of editing

Text editing decides what to say: from an automatic draft caption to a story quoting the source as "modern Korean (original)". Delivery editing decides how, through what and where it reaches people — the core emotion, the opening, the climax line, material and sensory devices, channels, and a closing question — so that the same fact becomes a record people remember.

How reliable is it — checks and review

  • Quotations: all 537 source lines quoted in the stories are matched letter for letter against the transcription (omissions must be marked …) — all match.
  • Dates: 1490 date expressions — Chinese and Japanese era names, Joseon reign years, the sexagenary (60-year) cycle and Western years — are cross-checked; 1 disagreement(s), where the original itself is inconsistent and says so in the notes. Years that come from the archive's own dating rather than the document are labelled as such.
  • Human review: 0 of 568 documents have been compared with the original images so far. Anyone can help — tick the documents you checked on the review page and send the downloaded reviews.csv as a GitHub issue. Until then every reading is marked as a draft.

How to cite

For a single document, press 인용 (cite) next to any search result: it copies the title, year, holding collection, archive item ID, viewer URL and your access date, plus a line naming this transcription and whether it has been reviewed. For the series and the tool, use the repository's CITATION.cff (GitHub: “Cite this repository”).
